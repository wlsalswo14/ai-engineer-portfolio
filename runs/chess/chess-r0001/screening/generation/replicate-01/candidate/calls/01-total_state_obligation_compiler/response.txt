# Exhaustive State-Semantics Obligation Ledger

Basis: supplied public task, champion artifact, hypothesis, and loop contract only. No hidden evaluator evidence, scores, tasksets, openings, or other runs were used.

## 2. Authoritative position state

The authoritative position must contain, and restoration must cover, all of:

1. 64-square piece placement.
2. Side to move.
3. Four castling-right bits.
4. En-passant target square or none.
5. Halfmove clock.
6. Fullmove number.
7. Repetition stack/counts for the real game path.
8. Any cached king locations, hash, or derived state, if introduced.
9. Search-local state must remain separate from position state.

Required invariants:

- Exactly one white king and one black king exist for every searchable legal position.
- No move may capture a king.
- The side that just moved may not leave its own king attacked.
- Board coordinates, side, flags, and clocks use one consistent representation.
- Position identity must include every field affecting legal moves, evaluation, or draw status.
- A child position must not alias mutable board or history storage belonging to its parent.
- Every child transition must have an exact inverse.

Champion assessment:

- `Position` stores board, side, castling, en-passant, halfmove, and fullmove fields.
- It has no repetition state.
- `king()` returns `-1` when a king is absent; `attacked()` then treats that as unattacked, violating the searchable-position invariant.
- `make()` copies the board but has no explicit reversible delta or unmake operation.

## 3. Position construction

### 3.1 Start position

- `Position.start()` must construct the exact standard initial position.
- White moves first.
- Castling rights are `KQkq`.
- En passant is absent.
- Halfmove clock is zero.
- Fullmove number is one.
- Repetition state begins with the initial position counted once.

Champion: the supplied start FEN is correct; repetition initialization is absent.

### 3.2 Six-field FEN

For `position fen`, construction must:

- Consume exactly six FEN fields.
- Decode eight ranks, each totaling exactly eight files.
- Accept only valid piece symbols and digits.
- Map rank 8 to internal rank 7 and rank 1 to internal rank 0.
- Decode `w` or `b` exactly.
- Decode `KQkq` or `-` exactly.
- Decode an en-passant coordinate or `-`.
- Preserve the supplied halfmove clock.
- Preserve the supplied fullmove number.
- Initialize repetition state for the constructed root.
- Establish valid king-state invariants before legal generation or search.
- Handle malformed input deterministically without silently converting it to another position.

Champion:

- Correctly maps valid board fields, side, castling bits, en-passant square, and clocks.
- Treats every side value other than `w` as black.
- Does not validate rank width, piece symbols, field count, castling syntax, en-passant rank, clock ranges, or king count.
- Can raise indexing or conversion errors on malformed input.
- Does not initialize repetition state.

### 3.3 Position replacement

- A new `position` command must replace the previous authoritative position.
- No prior board, en-passant state, castling state, clock state, or repetition history may leak into the new position.
- Search caches may only be reused when their keys remain semantically valid.
- `ucinewgame` must clear game-local repetition state and search heuristics/caches as required by the contract.
- Replaying a move list must produce the same state as applying those moves one at a time through the normal transition kernel.

Champion:

- `setpos()` reconstructs from `startpos` or FEN.
- It leaves the old `Engine.pos` untouched if parsing fails.
- It silently ignores an unmatched move token.
- It does not build replay repetition history.
- `ucinewgame` clears TT, history, and killers but does not reset `pos`.

## 4. UCI move replay

For every move token in `position ... moves`:

1. Parse exact coordinate notation.
2. Permit four-character moves and five-character promotion moves.
3. Match against the current legal move set.
4. Apply exactly one matching move.
5. Update all board and rule state through the authoritative kernel.
6. Advance repetition state.
7. Continue from the resulting position.
8. Reject or deterministically handle an unmatched token; never silently skip it.

Move notation must be reversible and exact:

- Files `a`–`h`.
- Ranks `1`–`8`.
- Promotion suffixes `q`, `r`, `b`, and `n`.
- Castling represented by king coordinate movement.
- No capture marker is included.

Champion:

- Replays by generating legal moves and comparing `notation(m)` with the token.
- Uses the same `make()` function as search.
- Silently continues after a token has no match.
- Does not update repetition state.
- Correctly maps its promotion codes to UCI suffixes.

## 5. Pseudo-legal generation

The generator must enumerate every pseudo-legal move for the side to move and no move for the opponent.

### 5.1 General

- Exclude empty squares and opposing pieces.
- Exclude captures of own pieces.
- Keep all coordinates inside the board.
- Generate deterministic move encodings.
- Do not generate king captures.
- Preserve special-move metadata unambiguously.
- Avoid duplicate moves.

Champion: ordinary piece traversal, bounds checks, own-piece exclusion, and deterministic encoding are present. King captures are filtered later by `legal()`, rather than excluded at generation time.

### 5.2 Pawns

For white, pawns advance toward increasing internal ranks; for black, decreasing ranks.

Required:

- One-square advance only into an empty square.
- Two-square advance only from the initial pawn rank.
- The intermediate and destination squares must be empty.
- Diagonal captures only against an opposing piece.
- En-passant generation only for a valid current target and valid opposing pawn.
- Promotion on reaching the last rank.
- All four promotions for quiet and capturing promotions.
- No ordinary pawn move beyond the promotion rank.
- Promotion moves retain capture, en-passant, clock, and legality semantics.

Champion:

- Correctly generates normal pushes, double pushes, captures, and four promotion choices.
- Generates en passant whenever the destination equals `p.ep`, without verifying the victim pawn, target rank, or target-square validity.
- Uses special flags for double push and en passant.

### 5.3 Knights, bishops, rooks, queens, kings

Required:

- Knights use all eight offsets.
- Sliding pieces stop at the first occupied square.
- A first opposing piece may be captured; no square beyond it is reachable.
- Bishops use diagonals.
- Rooks use orthogonals.
- Queens use both.
- Kings use the eight adjacent squares.
- King moves are subsequently filtered for attacked destinations.

Champion: these movement patterns and obstruction rules are present.

### 5.4 Castling generation

Castling is generated only when:

- The corresponding castling right exists.
- The correct king is on its home square.
- The corresponding rook is on its home square.
- Required intervening squares are empty.
- The king is not currently attacked.
- The king’s transit square is not attacked.
- The king’s destination square is not attacked.
- The move is not being generated in capture-only mode.

For queenside castling, the rook-side square must be empty even though the king does not cross it.

Champion checks these conditions for valid positions and uses rook-presence checks. Castling flags are trusted by `make()` rather than independently validated there.

## 6. King-safety legality

For every pseudo-legal move:

1. Apply it through the authoritative transition kernel.
2. Locate the moving side’s king.
3. Determine attacks by the opponent in the resulting position.
4. Reject the move if that king is attacked.
5. Restore the exact parent state before considering the next move.

This must correctly handle:

- Pinned pieces.
- Discovered attacks.
- Blocking moves.
- King captures.
- King adjacency.
- En-passant discovered checks.
- Castling occupancy and rook relocation.
- Promotions that reveal or block lines.
- Check evasions.
- Double check, where only king moves can be legal.

The attack detector must correctly implement:

- Pawn attacks with color-dependent direction.
- Knight attacks.
- King attacks.
- Sliding rook and queen attacks.
- Sliding bishop and queen attacks.
- Occupancy blocking.
- Board boundaries.

Champion:

- The attack geometry is generally correct.
- `legal()` applies each move and checks the original king.
- Castling checks the current, transit, and destination squares.
- Missing kings are treated as safe because `king()` returns `-1`.
- There is no explicit invariant preventing malformed or impossible king configurations from entering search.

## 7. Authoritative make/unmake kernel

The transition authority must be the sole interpreter of move effects.

### 7.1 Delta contents

Every reversible delta must record the old and new values for:

- Source square.
- Destination square.
- Captured square and captured piece.
- En-passant victim square and piece.
- Rook source and destination for castling.
- Promotion replacement.
- Side to move.
- Castling rights.
- En-passant target.
- Halfmove clock.
- Fullmove number.
- Repetition stack/count changes.
- Any cached king location or hash.

### 7.2 Ordinary moves and captures

Required:

- Source becomes empty.
- Destination receives the moving piece.
- Captured opposing piece is removed.
- Side changes exactly once.
- En-passant target is cleared unless the move is a valid double pawn push.
- Halfmove clock resets for every pawn move or capture.
- Otherwise halfmove clock increments by one.
- Fullmove number increments after Black moves only.
- Castling rights change when a king or relevant rook moves or when a relevant rook is captured.

### 7.3 En passant

Required:

- Destination receives the moving pawn.
- The opposing pawn immediately behind the destination is removed.
- The removed piece must actually be an opposing pawn.
- The move counts as a capture.
- The en-passant target is cleared afterward.
- Undo restores both destination and victim squares exactly.
- King-safety testing uses the post-capture board, including discovered lines.

Champion:

- Removes the expected victim square.
- Sets the captured marker to `'p'` unconditionally, even if the board does not contain the required pawn.
- Has no validation or explicit undo record.

### 7.4 Castling application

Required:

- Move the king from its home square to its destination.
- Move the rook from its corresponding corner to the correct adjacent square.
- Clear both castling rights for that side.
- Clear en passant.
- Increment the halfmove clock.
- Increment fullmove only after Black’s castle.
- Undo must restore all four involved squares and all rule fields.

Champion moves the rook based on destination flags and clears rights indirectly through the king move. It has no explicit reversible castle delta.

### 7.5 Promotion application

Required:

- Remove the pawn from its source.
- Place the promoted piece on the destination.
- Preserve the correct color.
- Support knight, bishop, rook, and queen.
- Handle both quiet and capture promotion.
- Reset the halfmove clock.
- Preserve capture and king-safety semantics.
- Undo restores the original pawn and captured piece.

Champion implements all four promotion pieces and resets clocks through pawn/capture handling.

### 7.6 Restoration

After every child search, rejected move, timeout, or exception:

- Board placement must equal the parent.
- Side, rights, en-passant, clocks, repetition counts, hashes, and king caches must equal the parent.
- Restoration must be LIFO for nested search.
- The root position must compare exactly equal before and after `think()`.
- Failed replay must not leave a partially applied move.
- No state-changing consumer may bypass the kernel.

Champion uses copied `Position` objects, so the ancestor board is normally not mutated. It nevertheless lacks an explicit make/unmake delta, repetition restoration, and validation of special flags.

## 8. Castling-right state

Castling rights are independent state and must be updated exactly:

- Moving White king clears `KQ`.
- Moving Black king clears `kq`.
- Moving White rook from `h1` clears `K`.
- Moving White rook from `a1` clears `Q`.
- Moving Black rook from `h8` clears `k`.
- Moving Black rook from `a8` clears `q`.
- Capturing a rook on any of those squares clears its corresponding right.
- Rights never reappear after being cleared.
- Undo restores the prior rights exactly.
- FEN rights are respected as input state, subject to rook/king occupancy checks for actual castling.

Champion clears rights based on source and destination corner indices, and king movement. It does not explicitly verify that a right-clearing capture is an actual rook capture.

## 9. En-passant state

Required:

- A valid double pawn push sets the square passed over.
- Every other move clears en passant.
- The target is available for only the immediately following ply.
- Replay and search use identical en-passant semantics.
- FEN en-passant state is preserved on construction.
- Invalid/stale en-passant fields cannot create fabricated captures.
- Repetition identity must distinguish en-passant only when it changes the legal-move possibilities.
- Undo restores the previous target exactly.

Champion sets the midpoint after any sixteen-square pawn displacement and clears it otherwise. Generation does not verify the required opposing pawn or all FEN validity conditions. TT keys use the raw `ep` value and no repetition key exists.

## 10. Clocks

Required after every applied move:

- Halfmove clock resets to zero after any pawn move.
- Halfmove clock resets to zero after any capture, including en passant and promotion captures.
- Otherwise halfmove clock increments by one.
- Fullmove number increments after every Black move.
- White moves do not increment fullmove.
- FEN clocks are preserved exactly at construction.
- Undo restores both clocks.
- Search branches must not alter the real game clocks.
- Replay must advance clocks exactly as normal moves do.

Champion’s ordinary clock updates are correct for generated valid moves. It has no restoration or draw-state use of the clocks.

## 11. Repetition state

Required:

- Initialize the root position with repetition count one.
- Record every successfully replayed game move.
- Record every search-line child only in that branch.
- Undo decrements/removes branch counts exactly.
- A repetition key includes:
  - Piece placement.
  - Side to move.
  - Castling rights.
  - Effective en-passant availability.
- Halfmove and fullmove counters are not part of the threefold-position identity, but remain part of full state.
- Search transposition reuse must not confuse positions with different repetition histories when repetition affects terminal scoring.
- `ucinewgame` clears the repetition history.
- Position replacement starts a fresh history rooted at the supplied position.

Champion has no repetition stack or counts. Its TT key contains board, side, castling, and raw en passant, but no repetition context or clocks.

## 12. Terminal positions

At every searchable node, the engine must distinguish:

- Checkmate: side to move is attacked and has no legal moves.
- Stalemate: side to move is not attacked and has no legal moves.
- Nonterminal position: at least one legal move exists.
- Draw-state conditions, when applicable under the chess rules represented by the state:
  - Repetition claims/automatic repetition thresholds.
  - Halfmove-rule claims or automatic thresholds.
  - Dead positions such as insufficient mating material.
- Terminal scoring must not be confused with move availability.
- If a terminal position has no legal move, output must use the deterministic UCI no-move sentinel rather than inventing an illegal chess move.
- If legal moves exist, `bestmove` must still be a legal UCI move even when the position is drawn or search scores it as a draw.

Champion:

- `search()` recognizes checkmate and stalemate from an empty legal list.
- `qsearch()` recognizes checkmate/stalemate in check and at its depth cutoff.
- `think()` returns `0000` when the root has no legal moves.
- It does not track or evaluate repetition, halfmove-rule status, or complete dead-position conditions.
- Its material-only evaluation shortcuts are heuristic scores, not complete terminal adjudication.

## 13. Search state and mutation boundaries

Required:

- Evaluation is read-only with respect to position state.
- Move ordering, history, killer moves, and TT entries are search metadata only.
- Search metadata must not alter board semantics.
- TT keys must contain all state relevant to the cached result.
- A cached move must be revalidated against the current legal move set before being used as a move.
- Search recursion must apply and restore transitions through the same authority used by replay.
- A timeout may leave search metadata partially updated, but never position state partially updated.
- Iterative deepening must retain the last fully completed legal result.

Champion:

- Evaluation reads position state only.
- TT, history, and killers are separate engine dictionaries.
- TT key omits clocks and repetition context.
- TT stores a move for ordering but does not use it as an unchecked final move.
- Search uses copied positions through `make()`.
- Iterative deepening preserves the previous `best` after a timeout.

## 14. Deadline-safe entry

For every `go movetime <ms>`:

1. Parse the time value deterministically.
2. Establish a monotonic deadline with output and cleanup reserve.
3. Generate a legal fallback move before entering unbounded search.
4. If the position has no legal moves, return the terminal sentinel promptly.
5. Do not begin a search iteration that cannot safely complete its required bookkeeping.
6. Preserve a legal fallback even if timeout occurs before depth one completes.
7. Use the same deadline throughout recursive search and quiescence.

Champion:

- Uses `time.perf_counter()`.
- Establishes a deadline at 82% of the requested time.
- Stores the first legal move as fallback.
- Uses a direct fallback for very small budgets.
- Does not explicitly reserve time for final output or perform a pre-search deadline check after fallback generation.

## 15. Deadline-safe search execution

Required checkpoints must cover:

- Search entry.
- Quiescence entry.
- Every root move.
- Every recursive child.
- Long move-generation and move-ordering loops.
- TT maintenance or clearing.
- Any potentially expensive evaluation or restoration operation.

Timeout handling must:

- Abort promptly.
- Preserve the last completed iteration’s legal move.
- Restore the root position exactly.
- Avoid emitting partial or duplicate output.
- Avoid allowing a broad unrelated exception to masquerade as normal timeout recovery.
- Keep all fallback generation bounded.

Champion:

- Calls `check_time()` at search and quiescence entry and every 32 node visits.
- Checks each root move.
- Catches `TimeoutError` and retains the prior completed result.
- Uses a broad `except Exception` around `think()` and falls back to the first legal move.
- Polling every 32 nodes does not establish a strict before-deadline guarantee for the work between checkpoints.
- Long local operations and final output are not explicitly deadline-accounted.

## 16. Deadline-safe exit and UCI response

Required:

- Emit exactly one line `bestmove <move>` per `go`.
- The move must be legal for the unchanged current position, unless the position has no legal moves and the no-move sentinel is required.
- Flush output immediately.
- Return before the supplied deadline.
- Do not process a later command before completing the current `bestmove`.
- `quit` must terminate cleanly.
- Search exceptions must not leave the engine without a response.

Champion:

- Prints and flushes one bestmove on normal and broad-exception fallback paths.
- The fallback itself calls `legal()` again, which is not separately deadline-bounded.
- A failure during fallback legal generation can prevent a response.
- The broad exception handler does not guarantee state restoration for any future mutable implementation.

## 17. Deterministic dependency order

The complete ledger must be realized in this order:

1. Establish the authoritative position schema and invariants.
2. Construct startpos and six-field FEN states.
3. Initialize repetition and clock state.
4. Parse and validate UCI move tokens.
5. Generate pseudo-legal moves.
6. Apply each candidate through the transition authority.
7. Test king safety and reject illegal candidates.
8. Restore exact parent state.
9. Use the same transition and legality path for UCI replay.
10. Update castling, en-passant, promotion, clocks, and repetition state.
11. Detect checkmate, stalemate, and applicable draw/dead-position states.
12. Enter iterative search with a legal fallback and deadline.
13. Preserve only fully completed search results.
14. Restore the root state.
15. Emit exactly one deadline-safe UCI response.

This is the single carried-forward obligation ledger for the supplied champion and public contract.
